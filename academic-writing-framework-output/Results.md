## Results

::: {.callout-note title="Results Logic"}
"We identify the **T**rajectory of change across the **R**egion. We quantify the **E**xtremes and the average **N**umbers, then validate it all with **D**ata visualization."
:::

::: {.callout-important title="Word Limit: 400--800 Words (Plus Figures)"}
The Results section is the "Truth." It must be objective, visual, and direct. Do not interpret *why* it happened (save that for Discussion); just state *what* happened.
- **T (Trajectory):** What is the overall trend line? (Up/Down/Stable).
- **R (Region):** Where is the change happening? (Spatial distribution).
- **E (Extreme):** What are the min/max values? (The outliers).
- **N (Number):** The hard stats. Mean, Median, Standard Deviation, Correlations.
- **D (Data Vis):** Reference your Figures and Tables explicitly.
:::

**Rule:** Every paragraph should reference a Figure (e.g., "As seen in Fig 3...").

_TREND_

---

#### T - TRAJECTORY (The Trend)

::: {.callout-note title="Rule"}
Describe the temporal direction of the data.

**Template:** *The temporal trajectory reveals a consistent [Direction] trend in [Variable].*
:::

::: {.callout-tip title="Example Sentences"}
- _The temporal analysis reveals a consistent upward trend in surface temperatures, increasing by approximately 0.3°C annually since 2010._
- _The time-series analysis indicates a monotonic decline in forest biomass, evidenced by a steady reduction in NDVI values post-2015._
- _Long-term hydrological data reveals a disturbing trend where the duration of flood events has increased by an average of 3 days per decade._
- _Meteorological records indicate a significant shift in climate patterns, with the frequency of 'Severe' drought events doubling since 2010._
- _The land use trajectory shows a rapid urbanization trend, characterized by a linear growth of 5% per year in built-up areas._
:::

---

#### R - REGION (The Spatial Pattern)

::: {.callout-note title="Rule"}
Describe the spatial distribution. Where is the "Hotspot"?

**Template:** *Spatially, the [Phenomenon] was concentrated in the [Specific Zone].*
:::

::: {.callout-tip title="Example Sentences"}
- _Spatially, this warming is not uniform but is distinctly clustered within the industrial zones, creating a sharp 'Hot Corridor' effect._
- _Geographically, this degradation is most pronounced at the reserve periphery, forming a characteristic 'Donut effect' while the core remains relatively intact._
- _The impact is spatially concentrated in the southern deltaic region, which experienced the deepest and most prolonged stagnation._
- _The spatial distribution of water stress is uneven, with the western administrative block consistently falling under the 'High' stress category._
- _Development intensity is spatially skewed, showing rapid densification primarily along the northern transit corridor._
:::

---

#### E - EXTREME (The Outliers)

::: {.callout-note title="Rule"}
Highlight the Minima and Maxima. The peaks and troughs.

**Template:** *Extreme anomalies were detected during the [Season/Year].*
:::

::: {.callout-tip title="Example Sentences"}
- _Extreme values were recorded during peak summer, with Land Surface Temperature (LST) reaching a maximum of 48°C, exceeding air temperature by 12°C._
- _The most severe impact was observed in fire-ravaged sectors, where the Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) plummeted to a critical low of 0.1._
- _Hydrological extremes reached their peak in 2020, with water levels recording a historical maximum depth of 3.5 meters in habitation zones._
- _The intensity of the drought peaked in 2015, where the Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) dropped to its lowest recorded value of 12._
- _The scale of transformation reached 100% conversion rates in the designated IT-Park zone, completely replacing prior land cover._
:::

---

#### N - NUMBER (The Statistics)

::: {.callout-note title="Rule"}
The hard descriptive statistics. Mean, Median, SD, R-squared.

**Template:** *Quantitatively, the analysis yields a net change of [Value].*
:::

::: {.callout-tip title="Example Sentences"}
- _Quantitatively, the urban-rural thermal gradient has widened to 4.2°C, with concrete surfaces contributing an additional 0.8°C per square kilometer._
- _Statistical analysis confirms a net forest loss of 540 hectares, while patch density has simultaneously increased to 12 patches per 100 hectares._
- _The devastation was extensive, resulting in damage to over 35,000 hectares of standing crop area across the affected districts._
- _The impact on productivity was statistically significant ($p<0.01$), with crop yield potential dropping by nearly 45% compared to the long-term average._
- _Quantitatively, this expansion has come at an environmental cost, resulting in a net reduction of 200 hectares of critical wetland ecosystems._
:::

#### D - DATA VIS (The Reference)

::: {.callout-note title="Rule"}
Explicitly link text to visuals (Figures/Tables).

**Template:** *As illustrated in Figure [X], the pixel-wise distribution confirms [Trend].*
:::

::: {.callout-tip title="Example Sentences"}
- _This pixel-wise thermal gradient is visually evident in the anomaly map presented in Figure 3, which highlights the correlation with impervious surfaces._
- _The transition from dense canopy to scrubland is systematically tracked in the degradation matrix shown in Figure 5._
- _The spatial extent of this recurrence is mapped in the inundation frequency analysis presented in Figure 4._
- _This cyclic pattern of moisture deficit is clearly captured in the temporal anomaly plot illustrated in Figure 6._
- _The detailed land cover transitions are summarized in the change detection matrix provided in Table 3._
:::

As illustrated in Figure 4, the pixel-wise distribution confirms the expansion of flood-risk zones.
